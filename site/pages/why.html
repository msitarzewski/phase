Why Phase & LUCID

Public compute infrastructure that no single provider controls.

Most "decentralized AI" projects build a proprietary token network first and treat privacy, simplicity, and local ownership as an afterthought. Phase starts from the opposite direction.

One substrate, many workloads

Phase (Apache-2.0)

The substrate handles only what every distributed compute network needs: persistent Ed25519 identities, Noise/QUIC peer discovery and NAT traversal, signed job manifests, streaming execution receipts, and content-addressed blob transfer.

It deliberately knows nothing about LLMs, weights, or prompts. All six library crates are licensed Apache-2.0 so anyone—even competing organizations—can adopt the protocol without legal friction.

LUCID & Plasm (Flagship Nodes)

LUCID (lucidd + Desktop app) is the GPU inference flagship built on Phase. It is licensed AGPL-3.0-or-later so improvements to the inference daemon stay open rather than disappearing into closed forks.

Plasm (plasmd, Apache-2.0) runs sandboxed WebAssembly modules on the exact same substrate, proving the protocol is genuinely workload-neutral. Future flagships (image synthesis, rendering, science) plug in as separate nodes rather than bloating LUCID.

Why no accounts, no billing, and no tokens

  • Start with people you actually trust: Friends, families, labs, and small teams already own powerful Macs and desktop GPUs that sit idle 90% of the day. They do not need a cryptocurrency or KYC gateway to share a local model safely.
  • Zero-friction local tooling: Because LUCID speaks the standard Ollama HTTP API on 127.0.0.1, your existing chat UIs, IDE assistants, and scripts work unchanged—and you can switch away anytime with zero lock-in.
  • Honest trust boundaries first: Cryptographic receipts today prove who ran a job and that the streamed bytes were not altered in transit. We label that honestly (remote-receipt-verified, remote-spot-checked, trusted-circle-sharded) instead of pretending a signature proves hidden GPU math.
