## Verdict

**Split into phases.** Approve a narrowly defined reduced-retention worker mode for implementation. Treat anonymous quota credentials as a separate single-provider pilot, and keep confidential GPU inference experimental until the complete execution path can be verified on a supported platform. The RFC correctly rejects ordinary sandboxes as protection from the serving host and warns that sharded activations carry information. Its threat matrix still promises too much. Blind issuance protects the credential linkage, disabling cache reuse reduces one source of residue, and attestation supports claims about a measured environment. Each claim has a different adversary and needs separate acceptance criteria. This review covers the working-tree RFC against the code at the commit above and the cited upstream sources. Hardware behavior was not tested. [RFC threat model](../../privacy-inference-rfc.md#2-threat-model-matrix)

## Subtle Attacks, Side Channels & Over/Under-Specification

- **Bearer theft and duplicate admission.** End-to-end Noise protects a correctly authenticated stream in transit. A bearer credential copied before redemption remains spendable by its holder. Two accepting replicas can each admit a credential when their spent state is local, and losing an in-memory ledger can reopen already-spent credentials. A timeout after admission adds ambiguity about whether execution happened. Specify theft assumptions, ledger ownership, the spend point, restart behavior, and retries before choosing epoch durations. [router/mod.rs:2152](../../../../../crates/lucidd/src/router/mod.rs#L2152) · [RFC 9577](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9577.html)
- **Small effective anonymity sets and active traffic correlation.** The meaningful set is the members who could plausibly make the same request in the observed period. Provider or model selection, rare quota classes, issuer-specific keying, refill timing, and intersecting periods of activity can reduce it to one. A provider can also vary response timing or chunking, strengthening correlation for a colluding observer. Desktop resends the full conversation on each turn. Changing identities per request leaves the previous text available as a linking signal. Local evidence records peer IDs, job hashes, model identifiers, and timestamps. Minimize correlation-bearing metadata, preserve uniform issuance policy, and keep job-level evidence local with bounded retention. [ChatPanel.svelte:54](../../../../../apps/lucid-desktop/src/lib/ChatPanel.svelte#L54) · [reputation.rs:148](../../../../../crates/lucidd/src/reputation.rs#L148) · [Privacy Pass anonymity sets](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9576.html#section-6) · [Tor correlation limits](https://support.torproject.org/about-tor/security/attacks-on-onion-routing/)
- **KV-cache reuse and residue need different controls.** Cross-request warm-cache timing can reveal shared prefixes or prior activity. Disabling prefix reuse addresses that channel only where the setting actually governs the path. Freed host or GPU allocations, checkpoints, child-process errors, swap, and crash dumps have different lifecycles. Separate a cache isolation claim from a forensic erasure claim, and scope retention to components whose behavior can be checked. [Pinned llama.cpp server cache](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/b12651/tools/server/server-context.cpp#L1485) · [Pinned llama.cpp KV clearing](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/b12651/src/llama-kv-cache.cpp#L388)
- **Inner HPKE adds complexity before it crosses a new trust boundary.** When the same serving daemon terminates Noise and decrypts HPKE, the serving operator still controls the plaintext. Defer inner encryption until it protects a real boundary, such as a transport-terminating queue or attested guest. Then authenticate the recipient key, bind the audience and mode to the request, specify independent directional encryption contexts and ordered frames, and authenticate the terminal result. HPKE leaves replay control to the application. A retained recipient key can expose previously captured ciphertext. ## Answers to Section 5 Open Design Questions (Q1–Q7)

1. **Q1 (Blind-Token Primitive).** Use RFC 9578 type `0x0001`, P-384/SHA-384 VOPRF, for a single issuer/verifier. The RFC’s 32-byte figure describes the nonce, and Ristretto255 differs from the specified Privacy Pass suite. Move to RFC 9578 type `0x0002` with RFC 9474 deterministic RSA-PSS only if independent public verification becomes necessary. Specify who can redeem a token and who owns spend state first. Evaluate Rust `voprf` and `blind-rsa-signatures` as primitive implementations. Audit status for the exact dependencies is unverified. Require RFC test vectors, malformed-input coverage, review of pinned dependencies and their unsafe or FFI boundaries, and review of entropy and constant-time behavior. Use a common, independently verifiable issuer key schedule and challenge profile so the issuer cannot silently partition the cohort with member-specific parameters. [RFC 9578 private profile](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9578.html#section-5) · [RFC 9578 public profile](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9578.html#section-6) · [RFC 9474](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9474.html#section-5) · [Rust VOPRF](https://github.com/meta/voprf) · [Rust blind RSA](https://github.com/jedisct1/rust-blind-rsa-signatures)

2. **Q2 (DoS & Spent-Token Store Bounds).** Use a bounded exact ledger keyed by audience, issuer key, and serial digest. Size it from total issuable stock across all grants and accepted epochs, with an aggregate cap in addition to per-grant caps. Bound frames and the pre-verification queue, verify the credential, reserve capacity, and atomically spend before dispatch. Keep entries until expiry and refuse admission at capacity. Require a shared authoritative ledger for replicas accepting the same audience, or start with one verifier and invalidate still-live credentials after state loss. A Bloom filter is optional as an accelerator with exact confirmation. Make issuance retries idempotent. Define at-most-once admission and treat a lost result after dispatch as indeterminate. Apply connection, queue, and execution limits independent of the ephemeral PeerID. [Current replay key and bounds, router/mod.rs:2152](../../../../../crates/lucidd/src/router/mod.rs#L2152)

3. **Q3 (Unlinkability vs Reputation / Revocation).** Revoke future issuance per grant, cap outstanding stock across overlapping windows, and support emergency disablement per key or audience. Anonymous tokens already issued to a revoked member remain valid until expiry unless their serials are known or the accepting key or audience is disabled. Balance short expiry against the need to avoid immediate refills that reveal timing. Keep provider reputation client-side. Use per-job cost ceilings, aggregate quotas, and worker concurrency limits for anonymous admission. Individual post-issuance revocation requires a deliberately different privacy contract. [RFC revocation question](../../privacy-inference-rfc.md#5-open-design-questions-for-multi-agent-review) · [Local reputation context, reputation.rs:133](../../../../../crates/lucidd/src/reputation.rs#L133)

4. **Q4 (Ephemeral PeerID & Relay Authz Binding).** Open a fresh restricted Noise connection, pin the serving peer identity end to end, and require the ephemeral request signer to match the delivering peer. Bind the token audience and protocol mode to the authenticated request. Define one canonical binding, since today’s stream job ID is already the manifest hash. A hash of public fields by itself leaves a captured bearer token transferable. If resistance to pre-redemption theft is a requirement, select a reviewed proof-of-possession profile that commits to a client-generated one-time key during blind issuance and proves possession at redemption. Keep credentials out of diagnostics, logs, and receipts in either profile. Verify concurrent admission and fail-closed negotiation before enabling the mode. [Current identity and manifest binding, router/mod.rs:2220](../../../../../crates/lucidd/src/router/mod.rs#L2220) · [RFC 9577 token format](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9577.html#section-2.2)

5. **Q5 (KV-Cache Scrubbing Reality Check).** At the pinned llama.cpp revision, `cache_prompt: false` skips the normal prefix-reuse branch. RAM prompt caches and checkpoints are separately controlled, and removing a KV sequence provides no guarantee that its underlying bytes were cleared. Lucid’s MLX adapter already passes `--prompt-cache-size 0`. The targeted upstream version evicts entries by dropping references. That gives no guarantee about allocator or Metal buffer contents. Use an isolated reduced-retention worker configuration, disable applicable reuse and checkpoint paths, and validate completion, cancellation, timeout, and crash per backend. Check both content-bearing artifacts and cross-request warm-cache timing. Account separately for copies in the HTTP stack, child runtime, desktop, swap, crash reporting, and GPU. Apply Rust zeroization to buffers it actually owns. 6. **Q6 (Layer 3 Attestation Supply Chain).** Use a replaceable verifier interface, with a separate `phase-attest` crate once a real platform justifies it. Pilot one validated CPU/GPU/driver configuration. Use signed, versioned appraisal policy with approved measurements, firmware and TCB minimums, rollback rules, and expiring revocation collateral. Bind a fresh verifier challenge, the guest encryption key, and the execution receipt key to the evidence. Verify that the CPU and GPU evidence covers the same guest and protected device session, including plaintext and I/O boundaries. Separate valid quotes can otherwise describe unrelated environments. State exactly which runtime, model, and configuration the policy measures. Issue the attestation signal only when the verified environment is bound to the job and verified output. A hostile client OS, compromised code inside the guest, and timing or availability leakage remain outside this claim. [Current receipt verification, router/mod.rs:1861](../../../../../crates/lucidd/src/router/mod.rs#L1861) · [RATS architecture](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9334.html) · [NVIDIA threat model](https://docs.nvidia.com/confidential-computing/virtual-machines/threat-model)

7. **Q7 (TailsOS / Tor Circuit Alignment).** Treat per-boot identity as a link for that boot. A new libp2p stream on a reused socket inherits the connection identity and Tor stream. The existing general TCP/QUIC swarm and the pinned TCP relay guide describe different layers. Make Tor-only egress an enforced mode with tested SOCKS isolation, fresh transport connections per isolation context, and no direct or discovery fallback. Separate issuance and redemption contexts and avoid just-in-time refill by default. Treat Desktop multi-turn chat as one linkable unit while it resends the conversation. RAM-only credentials reduce local residue while remote party observations and timing correlation remain. ## Concrete Recommendations (Priority Order)

1. Rewrite the threat matrix around distinct claims for credential unlinkability, transport privacy, reduced retention, and attested execution. Split ISP, relay, provider, client OS, serving host, guest, and observers at both ends. Name excluded observations and collusion assumptions in each claim. [RFC threat model](../../privacy-inference-rfc.md#2-threat-model-matrix)
2. Ship reduced retention on one validated backend first. Require fail-closed capability selection, no cross-request cache reuse in the supported configuration, and artifact and log checks on both normal and abnormal completion. [worker_llama/mod.rs:1143](../../../../../crates/lucidd/src/worker_llama/mod.rs#L1143)
3. Pilot one provider, one token profile, and one authoritative spend ledger. Validate quota bounds, replay across restarts, network isolation, unsupported-peer behavior, and the absence of unexpected additional recipients. Measure cohort fragmentation and correlation before making an anonymity claim. [router/mod.rs:2152](../../../../../crates/lucidd/src/router/mod.rs#L2152) · [Privacy Pass privacy considerations](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9576.html#section-6)
4. Add HPKE and a verifier when a real deployment crosses the corresponding trust boundary. Require fresh and bound evidence, a verified job receipt, and fail-closed behavior when policy or collateral is stale.
